# Complete sound response to expanded fitting

The expanded child mixture’s /W/ regression is concentrated in rating-0 and rating-1
vote loss, while its rating-2 loss improves slightly. The expansion also shifts the
sound distribution, especially toward /T/ and /N/. These verified descriptions
identify a testable training-weight hypothesis; they do not establish causality or
justify removing examples. All 23 sounds and all eight comparisons are retained.
Learner judgments remain disabled.

## Population and interpretation

This follows the [expanded fitting report](multiphone-lexical-expanded-heads-report.md)
and [conditional uncertainty report](multiphone-lexical-expanded-uncertainty-report.md)
under a [separate frozen protocol](multiphone-lexical-phone-response-protocol.md).
Loss responses use the same 5,654 original positions from 52 children aged 6–12.
Fitting composition covers 28,227 positions and all 50 speaker/word cells, both
inner fitting and outer refitting, both families/regimes, original/added/all
positions and all three declared age views. Counts are based on complete canonical
sequences, including same-sound repeats across words. They do not verify audio timing.
The machine report retains every class, five folds, 25 word cells and all 52 children
for every sound, including explicit zero support and null absent means.

## Next bounded modeling hypothesis

Test one global recording/sound-unit objective: give each occurrence weight
`1 / count(same canonical sound in its recording)`, normalized to mean one using
only the current fitting partition. Every position and rating remains; the rule is
identical for all sounds and does not use outer losses or per-sound tuning. This
asks whether counting every repeated occurrence equally overemphasizes repeated
sound groups. It is a hypothesis, not a demonstrated correction to /W/.

Keep whole-utterance word/speaker exclusions, original unweighted selection examples,
fixed model families and all original-child endpoints/fold checks. Compare against
the completed unweighted expanded controls. Preserve normalization/PCA choices to
isolate the training-loss change, with no class-prior correction. Before fitting,
separately declare the rule and verify all fitting repeated groups are complete,
weights use only fitting metadata, full row coverage and the exact all-one-weight
control trajectory. No training under that hypothesis has occurred here.

This differs from earlier inverse-rating-frequency weighting: it changes contribution
per recording/sound unit, without changing the relative weights of rating classes
within a position. It still changes the training distribution and may harm useful
rare-rating information. Retain those tradeoffs in all-sound and rare-event evaluation.
