# UltraSuite human-rating access: new verified lead

Checked September 28, 2026. The documented anonymous download server exposes real clinician pronunciation ratings for **/k/, /g/, and /r/**. This is a narrow external evaluation lead, not all-23-consonant produced-phone truth.

The live labels README identifies version1.2; its changelog dates the addition of pronunciation_scores to November11,2020. The two CSVs were actually acquired with exact listed sizes: UXSSD50,191bytes and UPX17,998bytes. Original bytes, observed hashes, source documents and commands are preserved in the [acquisition record](../../work/ultrasuite-annotation-audit-20260928/acquisition.json). No audio or ultrasound was acquired. The provider documents anonymous subset downloads, and the rating README explicitly licenses noncommercial research under CC BY-NC4.0. [Official access procedure](https://ultrasuite.github.io/download/)

The primary study describes768 main word instances from eight UXSSD children,24 shared UPX04M control items, eight experienced therapists and repeated presentations. Clinicians rated target goodness and, conditionally, an expected substitution using audio, spectrograms and ultrasound. Main items generally have one assigned rater; controls assess agreement and do not establish a healthy-child baseline. The study reports better agreement on velars than rhotics. These are ordinal multimodal judgments, not independently transcribed perceived-phone sequences. [Primary study, sections4 and8](https://arxiv.org/html/2103.00324v1)

The public [rating schema README](../../work/ultrasuite-annotation-audit-20260928/source-docs/pronunciation-scores-README) defines participant as **annotator**, not child. Targets are g/k/r metadata. Secondary scores are requested only when the primary score is below4; otherwise the paper describes a default1. The README's phrase “in those cases” is ambiguous, so preserve the original and record the paper-based interpretation. A secondary1 may be elicited or defaulted; derive provenance from the primary score, never the secondary value. Word boundaries were revised manually; the live changelog states phone boundaries remain forced alignments.

Published UXSSD ages identify seven children at6–10 and one at5years11months. UPX04M is listed at7years2months. These are child cohort ages; actual recording-session ages remain unknown. The UPX overview links to a separate age table: using the overview as an individual table caused the first audit to stop without results. Its source/declaration/trace remain preserved; a separately declared fresh route binds the linked speaker page. [UXSSD ages](https://ultrasuite.github.io/data/uxssd/), [UPX speaker table](https://ultrasuite.github.io/data/upx-spk/)

UX2020 is another accessible child-SSD source, with37 consented children aged5–12. A bounded independent reading checked live label inventory and a few tiny TextGrids; sparse phone points and word/syllable intervals did not establish target/perceived/error labels. Do not label these automatic or sparse annotations as clinical production truth. [Provider annotation description](https://ultrasuite.github.io/data/ux2020/)

The next decision depends on the bound annotation audit and verified word/audio joins. Keep this published evaluation resource separate from training and existing SpeechOcean development results. No score threshold, model evaluation, training recipe, learner feedback or clinical eligibility follows from access alone.
