      About the sound comparison

      When you choose Check sound, the current try is sent once to a speech-sound model running on this computer, not over the internet. The model compares four fixed versions of “bread” (B‑R‑E‑D, B‑W‑E‑D, B‑L‑E‑D and B‑E‑D). The page shows only which one the model fit best, or that none fit clearly.

      In engineering tests it picked BR for a licensed example recording of ‘bread’ and gave the same result when that recording was repeated. It gave no clear pick for ‘door’, silence and generated test signals. These are engineering checks, not independent labels of the sounds spoken. We haven't checked it against independently labelled real examples of W, L or no R in this word, or established validity for children's speech.

      It is offered only when Adult (18+) is chosen for Who is practicing? and both boxes are ticked. That choice is your own and is not verified. With a child age or no choice, nothing is sent for sound comparison.

      It gives no scores, grades, diagnoses or practice advice. Word check and the sound comparison are separate: each sends its own temporary copy, and only when you choose it. The comparison processes a separate temporary copy locally after the explicit Check sound action. Audio is deleted after confirmed cleanup; private processing diagnostics can remain. An uncertain cleanup retains the private stage for investigation and pauses both kinds of processing.

                  About the example recording

                  About this build

                  This is an engineering prototype for local development. It is not for clinical or production use.

                  Word check is optional. When it is on and you choose Check this try, that one recording is sent to a speech recognition model running on this computer, not over the internet. It reports whether the model detected speech and whether its recognition of the whole word matched the word shown. Recognition can miss words that were said clearly and can match words that were not, so neither result is a judgment of pronunciation. Word check does not check individual sounds, give scores or diagnoses, or measure progress. Whole-word recognition is experimental, and how well it works for adults' or children's speech has not been established. Who is practicing is your own choice; the page does not verify age.

                  A check also describes the recording itself: its length, its average level and how many samples reached the maximum level. These describe the recording, not the speaker. Once a try has a result it cannot be checked again, unless the check did not finish; record a fresh try instead.

                  Up to two tries for the current word are held in this browser tab's memory only, so you can play them back and compare them. The page does not store them on disk. Both tries and their results are cleared when you choose another word, change who is practicing, word check or sound comparison settings, choose Clear tries, switch away from the page or close it. The session count is kept in memory only and resets when you leave or reload the page.

                  The computer is built to delete each checked recording when the check finishes, but deletion cannot be guaranteed, for example if the computer stops part way; if cleanup cannot be confirmed, word check pauses. If you stop waiting or leave the page, the computer may still finish a check in the background; no result is shown.

                  The mirror shows the camera picture on this screen only. Show a mouth outline is a separate optional local face model. It estimates visible mouth position, can lose track or be wrong, and gives no speech, tongue, depth, identity or diagnostic finding. Camera frames and landmarks are not saved or uploaded. The local worker blocks external network requests, including attempts by the model library to send telemetry. Camera and face analysis start only after you choose them; analysis turns off when you change words or settings, clear tries, turn off the camera or leave the page.

                  Example recordings are existing public recordings shared under their own licenses; details are listed with each one. Speaker ages are not known. The words and wording have had engineering review only.
